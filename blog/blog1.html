How I Built My Portfolio Website
March 24, 2025

Starting from Scratch: The Vision

When I set out to build my portfolio website, I knew I wanted something that was truly my own—no templates, no frameworks, just a blank canvas. My goal was to create a site that reflected my personality and work, while keeping the user experience clean and minimalistic. I started by sketching ideas in Figma, focusing on a layout that was easy to navigate and visually appealing without being overwhelming.

One of the first questions I asked myself was: what do I want visitors to feel when they land on my site? I wanted the experience to be calm, focused, and professional, but also to have a touch of personality. I spent time browsing other portfolios and design inspiration sites, taking notes on what worked and what felt cluttered. This research phase helped me clarify my own design values and set the tone for the rest of the project.

Designing in Figma: Minimalism and Dark Mode

I chose a dark color palette from the start. Dark backgrounds are not only easier on the eyes, but they also allow content—like text, images, and buttons—to stand out. In Figma, I experimented with different shades of gray and subtle accent colors, making sure the design felt modern and professional. I kept the number of colors minimal, using contrast and spacing to guide the user's attention.

Figma was invaluable for quickly iterating on layouts. I created multiple wireframes, testing different arrangements for the hero section, about, skills, and projects. I paid special attention to typography, choosing fonts that were both readable and stylish. I also designed a consistent set of buttons and tags, ensuring that every interactive element felt cohesive. The dark theme not only looked good but also helped reduce eye strain during late-night coding sessions.

Building the Foundation: HTML, CSS, and Responsiveness

With the design in hand, I began coding the site from scratch using HTML and CSS. I paid special attention to responsiveness, ensuring the layout looked great on both desktop and mobile devices. I used flexbox and grid for layout, and kept my CSS modular and organized. Every section—from the hero to the projects grid—was built to be clean and easy to read.

One challenge was making sure that the site loaded quickly and looked good on all screen sizes. I used media queries to adjust padding, font sizes, and grid layouts for smaller devices. I also made sure to test the site on multiple browsers and devices, catching small bugs and inconsistencies early. By building everything from scratch, I had full control over the codebase, which made it easier to optimize and maintain.

Adding the Photography Page: Challenges and Solutions

One of my favorite features is the photography page. Initially, I uploaded high-resolution images directly, but I quickly noticed the page loaded slowly, especially on mobile. This was a problem: large images can ruin the user experience. To fix this, I used ImageMagick to batch downsize my photos, reducing their file size dramatically without sacrificing quality. I also used modern image formats like webp for even faster loading. The result: a gallery that loads quickly and looks great on any device.

Before optimizing, some images were over 5MB each, which made the gallery nearly unusable on slower connections. I wrote a simple script to automate the downsizing process with ImageMagick, converting all images to a maximum width of 1600px and compressing them to a reasonable quality. I also implemented lazy loading for the gallery images, so only the photos in view are loaded initially. This made a huge difference in perceived performance and user experience.

Performance Tweaks and Small Details

Performance is more than just fast images. I minified my CSS and JavaScript, optimized font loading, and made sure to lazy-load images where possible. I also added subtle animations and hover effects to make the site feel interactive without being distracting. Small details—like a live viewer count, smooth scroll indicators, and a responsive footer—add polish and professionalism.

One detail I enjoyed implementing was the animated typing effect for my job title, which cycles between "Data Analyst" and "Software Engineer." I also added a scroll progress indicator and interactive project cards that respond to hover. These touches may seem minor, but they contribute to a more engaging and memorable experience for visitors. I believe that attention to detail is what sets a great portfolio apart from a good one.

Footer and Finishing Touches

The footer is often overlooked, but I wanted mine to be both functional and stylish. I included social links, a back-to-top button, and a live viewer count. The footer only appears when you scroll down, keeping the focus on the main content but always accessible when needed.

I also made sure the footer was responsive and visually balanced. The social icons are spaced evenly, and the back-to-top button only appears when you reach the bottom of the page. I added a subtle blur and border to the footer background, making it stand out just enough without drawing too much attention. These finishing touches help tie the whole site together.

Showcasing Projects

My projects section is the heart of the portfolio. Each project card includes a title, description, tech stack, and links to GitHub or live demos. I used a grid layout for easy browsing, and made sure each card is visually distinct but consistent with the overall theme. As I continue to build new projects, I can easily add them to this section.

For each project, I wrote a short summary and highlighted the technologies used. I also included links to the source code and live demos where possible. This not only showcases my technical skills but also demonstrates my ability to communicate and document my work. I plan to keep this section updated as I complete new projects and learn new technologies.

Continuous Improvement

Building this portfolio has been a journey of learning and iteration. I'm always looking for ways to improve performance, accessibility, and design. Whether it's adding new features, optimizing images, or tweaking the layout, I see my portfolio as a living project that grows with me.

Looking ahead, I want to experiment with new web technologies, such as serverless functions and advanced CSS techniques. I'm also considering adding a blog (like this one!) to share my experiences and insights with others. The process of building and refining my portfolio has taught me a lot about web development, design, and the importance of user experience.

If you have any feedback or want to collaborate, feel free to reach out through my contact links. Thanks for reading!
