# Ranking and Rating Every Class I Took at UC Berkeley

During my four years at **UC Berkeley** studying **Computer Science & Data Science**, I took over 20 rigorous technical courses spanning software systems, machine learning theory, data engineering pipelines, advanced probability, and physics.

Berkeley courses have a notorious reputation for intense problem sets, massive 40-hour projects, and brutal exams. Now that I've graduated (Class of 2026, 3.75 GPA), I wanted to look back and give an honest, unvarnished review and rating of every single class I completed—including difficulty, workload, key takeaways, and whether they were worth the grind.

## Top 5 Recommendations for Cal CS/DS Students

1. **CS 170 (Algorithms):** Do not graduate without taking this. Essential for developing structured problem-solving intuition and succeeding in technical interviews. Even if you can learn yourself, makes life easier by starting somewhere.
2. **CS 189 (Introduction to ML):** Getting your foot through the door, learning the basics in today's age.
3. **DATA C101 (Data Engineering):** Hands-on experience with modern big data pipelines (Spark, streaming, distributed architectures).
4. **DATA 140 (Probability):** The best math/probability course offered at Berkeley (there are alternatives).
5. **DATA 100 (Data Science):** The class I actually learned and enjoyed taking the most.

Overall, the Berkeley curriculum pushed me beyond what I thought I was capable of. The combination of deep theoretical rigor with large-scale systems implementation gave me the confidence to design, debug, and ship complex software in any domain. But learning in school is barely enough for real job applications and interviews. You have to network through clubs, hackathons, research, etc. But overall, Berkeley was an amazing experience that I would not trade for anything.
