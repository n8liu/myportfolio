Why I Love Data Science
April 2, 2025

The Intersection of Curiosity and Impact

Data science has always felt like magic to me. At its core, it is the art of translating raw, chaotic numbers into structured stories that guide decisions, predict outcomes, and uncover hidden truths about human behavior. Unlike traditional software engineering, where the bounds of code are deterministic, data science thrives in the domain of probability, patterns, and discovery.

My interest started when I began analyzing small public datasets in python. I realized that a few lines of code utilizing libraries like Pandas and Matplotlib could reveal insights that were invisible to the naked eye. That "aha!" moment—where a clean line chart or cluster visualization solves a complex question—is what keeps me hooked.

Power of Data Visualization

They say a picture is worth a thousand words, and in data science, a good visualization is worth a million data points. The ability to take millions of rows of telemetry and distil it into an intuitive, interactive dashboard (like the stats dashboard on this portfolio!) is extremely satisfying. It bridges the gap between technical complexity and business communication.

During my coursework at UC Berkeley, specifically in DATA 100, we learned that visualization is not just the final step of an analysis; it is a critical exploratory tool. A simple scatter plot can instantly reveal non-linear relationships or data entry errors that summary statistics like mean and variance completely hide.

Continuous Journey of Learning

The field of data science is evolving at a breakneck pace. From classical regression models and Bayes estimators to deep learning neural networks and Large Language Models, there is always something new to learn. Balancing the theoretical foundations of linear algebra and probability (classes like EECS 127 and DATA 140) with the practical aspects of databases (CS 186) makes the discipline challenging but highly rewarding.

As I continue my studies and projects, I am excited to apply these techniques to real-world challenges, whether optimizing web application latency, predicting user engagement, or analyzing photography compositions.
